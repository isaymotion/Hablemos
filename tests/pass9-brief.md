## C. Numbers, dates and time (`#/numbers`)
Port `js/numbers.js` (tabs learn / quiz / clinic; quiz modes Read: numeral or label → Spanish words, and Listen: hear the Spanish → pick the label; tricky rows asked twice as often). Score keys: `state.done.numbers['<groupId>:read' | '<groupId>:listen']` (group `mixed` = all), `state.done.numbers.clinic`; setting `numGroup`.
Data `js/data/numbers.js`: `window.ES_NUMBERS = { groups: [{ id, title, note, rows: [[label, es, tricky?]] }], clinical: [[es, en, question, answer, [wrong, wrong, wrong]]] }`. `label` is what the learner reads ("16", "2:30 p.m.", "3/4/2026", "½ tableta", "988"); `es` is how it is said, plain Spanish; `tricky` = 1 marks the forms learners get wrong. Groups (10 to 16 rows each, about 130 rows in all):
`n0-15`; `n16-29` (dieciséis, veintiuno, veintidós, veintitrés, veintiséis: one word, accents); `tens` (30 to 99: treinta y uno; sesenta / setenta); `hundreds` (cien vs ciento, quinientos, setecientos, novecientos; doscientas with feminine nouns); `thousands` (mil with no "un", years 1998 / 2026, un millón de); `ordinals` (primero / primer, tercer, piso, first visit, primera vez); `clock` (es la una, son las dos y media, y cuarto, menos cuarto vs "faltan quince para", de la mañana / tarde / noche, mediodía, medianoche; note the 24-hour clock on appointment slips); `days-months` (lowercase in Spanish; el lunes, los lunes); `dates` (el primero de mayo, el tres de marzo de dos mil veintiséis; note: many patients write day/month, so 3/4 may mean 3 April: confirm); `spans` (hace quince días = two weeks, ocho días = a week, quincena, cada tercer día (Mexico: every other day), hace un año y medio, a los diecinueve años); `doses` (media pastilla, una pastilla y media, dos veces al día, cada ocho horas, por la mañana y por la noche, miligramos; decimals "cero punto cinco" vs "cero coma cinco"; ordinary schedules only, nothing that reads as overdose guidance); `phones-scales` (988 said digit by digit, phone numbers in pairs, del cero al diez, percent, room and bed numbers).
`clinical`: 40 sentences a patient, relative or nurse might say with a number, date or time in it (sleep hours, how long symptoms have lasted, age at onset, appointment time, doses as prescribed, drinks per day, date of birth, days in hospital, 988). `question` in English, `answer` and three plausible wrong answers in English (the classic confusions: sesenta / setenta, quince días, dos / doce, 3/4, una y media). Mix usted answers from patients and nurse lines.
The audio pack records every `es` and every clinical `es`.
